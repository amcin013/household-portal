Getting the data

Electricity

In deregulated Texas the meter belongs to the wires company, not whoever you buy power from — so the readings come from Smart Meter Texas, not TXU. Register once with your ESIID, then export. It holds 13 months of 15-minute interval data, and offers both a CSV and a Green Button XML. Either works here.

This is worth doing whoever you buy from: switch retailer and the history stays, because the meter did not change.

Gas and water

Fewer of these offer Green Button. Look for "usage history" or "download" in the account portal; if all you can get is a table on screen, typing one number off each bill is enough — it is twelve numbers a year.

What it gets you

A bill going up $50 means one of two completely different things. Recording usage separates them:

  • Rate — dollars per kWh over time. This is what tells you whether shopping for a new plan is worth the trouble.
  • Volume — how much you actually drew, independent of price.
  • Per day — so a 33-day bill and a 28-day bill compare honestly.

Readings land on the bill for the month they cover, so a month with no bill recorded is skipped and told to you rather than invented.
